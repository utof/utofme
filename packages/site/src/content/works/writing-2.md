Production post body.

Iterative thinking is the practice of treating every output as a draft. Instead of aiming for a finished product on the first attempt, you release something workable, observe how it behaves in context, and revise. The feedback loop compresses the distance between intention and outcome.

Software development has internalized this rhythm through short release cycles and continuous integration. The discipline transfers to writing, design, and research: publish an early version, gather reactions, and improve. The important thing is that each iteration is complete enough to be useful rather than merely promising.

The risk is that iteration becomes an excuse to never finish. A useful heuristic is to ship when removing any further feature would reduce value rather than increase clarity. At that point the loop closes and the work stands on its own.
