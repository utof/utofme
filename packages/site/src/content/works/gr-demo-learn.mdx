A beginner-friendly general-relativity lab in a Julia Pluto notebook. {/* src: README.md:3 */} You start with a straight worldline, then drag a launch point around a Schwarzschild black hole and scrub the solved orbit while the effective-potential marker moves. {/* src: README.md:4-6 */}

SymPy derives the curvature again and SciPy solves a different reduced system; both agree with the Julia samples to about 10⁻¹². {/* src: README.md:48-49; artifacts/02_independent_check.executed.ipynb cell 7 output (max differences 1.29e-12 radius, 6.54e-13 angle) */} The scope is narrow on purpose: test particles in the equatorial plane, no spin, and integration stops at r = 2.02M. {/* src: README.md:89-92 */}
